The Line

Three products. Insoles, slides, headbands. We do not offer what we do not print, so the list is short by rule rather than by roadmap — and anything parked, unproven or bought in is not on it.

Every item passes the same test the insole did: **would we use this ourselves, and does the print do something an injection-moulded version couldn't?** If the answer to the second one is no, the honest move is to buy the moulded version and not make it. That question is why this list is short.

Bull-Grade

A joke that turned into a spec. Bull-Grade is our qualified materials list, and it is short on purpose. We print nothing else. If a product needs a material outside this list it needs a qualification run before it can be sold — a bench decision, not a marketing one.

CodeMaterialWhat it is for
BG-01TPU 95AThe firm one. Footbeds, insoles and anything that has to hold an arch under load.
BG-02TPU 80ASuspended. The machine vendor's own guide says 85A and softer should not be fed through our extruder at all, so this was listed as qualified against hardware that will not run it. It comes back when a coupon proves otherwise, not before.
BG-01bTPU 90AThe skin-contact one. White is doctrine, not preference: a white part shows dirt and wear immediately. Bull Button and any wear surface.
BG-03PEBAThe performance material — lighter and springier than TPU. Listed, not yet qualified on our machines: no coupon has been run for it. Nothing ships in it until one has.

Two entries here are honest about not being finished, and that is the point of having a list: qualifying a material and wanting to use it are different decisions, and only one of them is ours to make by deciding.

Four finishes

Gold is the signature and stays the hero — insoles ship in it. Three supporting finishes let the slides read as everyday footwear rather than trophies.

CodeFinishWhere it goes
CW-01GoldThe signature. Loud on purpose. Insoles.
CW-02GraphiteEquipment finish, matte near-black. Slides.
CW-03BoneThe contrast run. Limited only.
CW-04TitaniumIndustrial grey for premium runs, in PEBA.

One finish per object. No two-tone prints, no colour-change gimmicks. Where a bull mark needs to contrast — a gold bull on a black slide, a black bull on a white one — it is printed as its own part and seated flush into a pocket, so the two colours are two prints that never meet on one plate.

Bitcoin orange is a filament we do not stock. ₿ appears as geometry, never as a colour.

<p class="pull">Rainbow, silk, glitter and colour-shift filaments print beautifully and make the object look like a hobby print — which is the exact impression this whole brand is built to avoid.</p>

Three drops, not a catalogue

A full-catalogue launch reads as a dropshipper. Three drops read as a lab with a point of view. Each one gets its own week, its own story and its own verdict.

  • Drop 001 — Bull Insoles. Live. The thesis: a lattice tuned per foot with

no tooling cost, because there is no tool.

  • Drop 002 — Bull Slides. In the lab. Three printed parts, no adhesive in

the load path.

  • Drop 003 — Bull Headbands. In the lab. The Bull Button sewn through

spacer mesh, with its materials gate still open.

After that: reprints for worn pairs and custom runs on the same three. Owning the file is the whole advantage — a worn pair is a repeat order rather than a lost customer. There is no wave four written down, because writing one down is how a lab talks itself into a catalogue.

Editions stay small because we choose to, not because we can't print more. That distinction has to stay true or the scarcity becomes theatre.

What we won't sell

Anything carrying a safety burden we can't back with testing. No orthotic claims, no protective footwear, no climbing, no safety toe. A footbed that fails is an uncomfortable afternoon; those other things fail differently. The line is there on purpose and it does not move for a good quarter.
