BEST IN BULL™ is not a slogan we print on everything. It is a gate, and most of
what comes off the plate does not clear it.

A part carries the stamp when it can produce all five of these. Not describe
them — produce them.

## 1 · It is re-derivable

Every number the geometry depends on lives in a spec file, and the spec plus the
generator reproduces the mesh **byte for byte**. Nothing lives in a command line
or in somebody's memory. If we cannot rebuild it identically from source, it
does not ship.

## 2 · It is measured, not described

The dimensions we publish are cut out of the mesh that goes to the slicer, not
copied from the design notes. A part that is secretly a flat slab, or a heel cup
that never actually rose, gives itself away in a cross-section. So we take the
cross-section.

## 3 · It is manifold and it is one piece

Watertight, zero degenerate triangles, verified. **One solid body** — we do not
ship a two-part insert and we do not ship a mesh that is two overlapping shells
hoping the slicer sorts it out.

## 4 · It prints without support

Every pocket wall is drafted so each layer is carried by the one beneath it. On
Drop 001, 1.2 % of downward-facing area sits past 45° from vertical and 0.06 %
past 55°. Nothing in the part prints into air.

## 5 · The failure modes are written down before the print starts

Not after. The build sheet names what is most likely to go wrong, in order,
with the setting that addresses each one. For Drop 001 the top of that list is
first-layer adhesion, because a large share of the footprint meets the plate as
narrow ribbons and under-squished TPU will not hold them.

---

## What the stamp is not

It is not a claim that a part is good for your feet. We do not make medical or
therapeutic claims, and no amount of engineering rigour on our side turns into a
clinical claim.

It is a claim that we know exactly what we made, that we measured it, and that
you can check the numbers yourself.

A stamp everything earns is decoration. This one has to cost something.
