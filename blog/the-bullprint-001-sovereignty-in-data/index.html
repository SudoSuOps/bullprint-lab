THE BULLPRINT #001 — Sovereignty in Data

2026-08-15

For most of the internet's history, we traded sovereignty for convenience. Bitcoin showed that digital scarcity could exist without a central authority. The next question is whether our data can work the same way.

For most of the internet's history, we traded sovereignty for convenience.

Our identity.

Our conversations.

Our purchases.

Our movements.

Our preferences.

Our digital history.

We created the data.

Someone else owned the rails beneath it.

Bitcoin introduced a radically different idea: a digital asset you can hold without asking an institution for permission.

But the larger principle goes beyond money.

What if your data worked the same way?

AI is making this question urgent.

As intelligence becomes increasingly personalized, the data surrounding an individual becomes more valuable — not merely financially, but as an extension of identity.

Your history teaches machines about you.

Your decisions reveal your preferences.

Your conversations contain pieces of how you think.

In an AI-native world, sovereignty cannot stop at money.

We should be building toward a future where individuals can control their data, decide where it lives, determine who receives access, and revoke that access when they choose.

Not because every institution is malicious.

Because ownership is different from permission.

Bitcoin demonstrated that digital scarcity could exist without requiring a central authority to maintain the ledger.

Perhaps one of the next great challenges is bringing that same philosophy to our digital selves:

Your keys. Your data. Your intelligence. Your choice.

The internet taught us to upload ourselves.

The next internet should teach us how to own ourselves.
